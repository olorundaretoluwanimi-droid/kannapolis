# Research notes — What Happened to Kannapolis?

## People and their own words (public domain)

**Alice Caudle**, spinner, Cannon Mills (Concord, Plant No. 1), interviewed by Muriel L. Wolff, Concord NC, 2 September 1938. U.S. WPA Federal Writers' Project, Life Histories 1936–39, Library of Congress (transcript via ANCHOR, State Library of NC: https://www.ncanchor.org/anchor/alice-caudle-talks-about). U.S. government work, public domain.
- "Law, I reckon I was born to work in a mill. I started when I was ten year old and I aim to keep right on jest as long as I'm able."
- "Yessir, when I started down here to plant No. 1, I was so little I had to stand on a box to reach my work."
- "If I'd a-been a man no telling how far I'd-a gone."
- "I've worked for the Cannon Mills now for over thirty years," (proudly) — a pin at the 25-year supper, Mary Ella Hall, Kannapolis.
- "'Well Sir,' I said to him, 'in all these thirty years this is the first time the Cannon Mill ever offered me anything -- are you right sure they're not a-going to take hit off my pay?"
- "Charles Cannon made a fine speech and give out the pins to us. He told about the way young'uns used to stand on boxes to work — the way I done."
- "As long as I can work and talk and laugh, I'm happy," … "and I get to do plenty of all of them."
- Facts in the text: 47 years old in 1938; shift 7:00–3:30 with half an hour for lunch; $31.00 for two full weeks of five days; rent $6.00 a month in a company village; an agreement that if a hand quit one mill, the other mills in town would not hire him.

**Lewis W. Hine, 1912 (National Child Labor Committee, LoC; public domain)**
- "Herman Parker, said six years old, and he and others said he had worked in the Cannon Mills, Kannapolis, N.C." (caption title: "The Logical Conclusion of the Laissez Faire Policy")
- "A group of children attending the public school, Kannapolis, N.C., every one of which said he or she had worked in the Cotton Mills there. The two smallest boys said ten years old."
- "The boy 'Otie Honeycott' (his mother could not spell his name) said he had been doffing two years in the Cannon Mill." (Concord)
- "Some of the housing conditions of the workers in the Cannon Mills, Concord, N.C., to contrast with the homes and gardens sometimes shown from the 'show mills' of the state."

**Eleanor Roosevelt** visited the Kannapolis YMCA, August 1942 (Salisbury Post, from her "My Day" column of 15 Aug 1942); NARA photo 196166.

## Timeline (to source in FACTS.md)
- 1887 James W. Cannon organizes a cotton mill in Concord (NC marker L-55; Wikipedia).
- 1898 switch from Cannon cloth to towels, the first towels produced in the South (NCDNCR marker text).
- 1906 Cannon buys farmland (808 acres per NC Research Campus timeline / Wikipedia; other sources 600 or 1,008) on the old Salisbury–Charlotte wagon road; mill village named Kannapolis. Name: "Cannon + polis"; "city of looms" is popular but contested (historian Gary Freeze, Salisbury Post).
- 1912 Lewis Hine photographs child workers in Kannapolis and Concord.
- By 1914 largest towel and sheet manufacturer in the world (Wikipedia; NCpedia says largest US towel producer by end of WWII).
- 1921 James W. Cannon dies; son Charles A. Cannon takes over. 1928 nine companies merged into Cannon Mills Co.
- 1934 General Textile Strike (September); Daily Worker image 22 Sep 1934.
- 1938 Alice Caudle interview; 25-year supper at Kannapolis.
- 1942 Eleanor Roosevelt at the YMCA.
- Sanborn maps of Kannapolis 1926, 1932, 1943 (1943 index: population figure on sheet).
- 1971 Charles Cannon dies (2 April 1971; NCpedia).
- 1974 first union vote lost; 1985 lost (ACTWU); 1991 and 1997 votes set aside by NLRB for company intimidation; June 1999 UNITE wins (first union victory; ~5,100 workers).
- 1982 David Murdock buys Cannon Mills (leveraged buyout). 1984 Kannapolis incorporates (largest unincorporated community in US until then).
- 1985/86 sold to Fieldcrest → Fieldcrest Cannon. Pension: plan closed, assets moved to annuities with an insurer that later failed; ~30% benefit cut (insurer unnamed in source — do not name).
- 1997 Pillowtex buys Fieldcrest Cannon. 2000 Chapter 11; 2002 emerges; 30 July 2003 closes: 4,340 jobs in Cabarrus and Rowan counties, the largest single-day layoff in NC history.
- Plant No. 1 (5.8 million sq ft, about the size of the Pentagon) demolished by D.H. Griffin for Castle & Cooke (Murdock); implosions 2006.
- North Carolina Research Campus on the site (Murdock).
