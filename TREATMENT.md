## The question

On 30 July 2003, 4,340 people in Kannapolis, North Carolina, lost their jobs on the same day — the largest layoff in the
state's history. For almost a century the town had not had a mayor, a council or a police chief of its own: it had a
company. How does a town get built around one product, one family and one mill — and what is left when the mill goes?

## Three structures considered

1. **Strict chronology** 1887 → 2003 → today. Clear, but the ending is known from the title and the middle sags.
2. **The question answered witness by witness** — the child (Hine, 1912), the spinner (Alice Caudle, 1938), the First
   Lady (1942), the voters (1974–1999), the buyer (1982), the last shift (2003).
3. **Cold open on the decisive day, then rebuild on one clock** — 30 July 2003, then back to an empty field in 1906,
   forward through the maps and the people to the same day, and on to what stands there now.

**Chosen: 3, carried by 2.** The film opens on 30 July 2003 and the number 4,340; rewinds to the 1906 farmland; then
seven chapters, each led by a witness or a document, with the Sanborn maps of 1926, 1932 and 1943 as the town's clock
(the same streets, filling in), and returns to the opening day before a final chapter on the site today.
